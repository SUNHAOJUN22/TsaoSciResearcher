# 76 Standard Inventive Solutions / 76 个标准发明解

# Class 1 — Building and destruction of Su-Fields (13)

## 1.1 Synthesis of a substance-field system (8)

**1.1.1 — Synthesis of a Su-Field.** Complete an incomplete model by introducing the missing substance and/or field.

**1.1.2 — Improve interaction with an additive inside an object.** Create an internal complex Su-Field by adding a controllable/property-changing additive to `S1` or `S2`.

**1.1.3 — Improve interaction with an additive in the system.** Create an external complex Su-Field by adding a separate third substance that modifies the interaction.

**1.1.4 — Use the environment.** Obtain the needed third substance from the environment or supersystem instead of adding a dedicated component.

**1.1.5 — Modify the environment.** Replace, decompose, phase-change, or minimally dope the environment to generate the required substance/property.

**1.1.6 — Provide a minimal/controlled action through excess plus removal.** Apply a conveniently large action and remove/neutralize the excess with another substance or field.

**1.1.7 — Preserve maximum action but redirect it.** When strong action on the target is forbidden, apply the maximum action to an attached/intermediate object that transfers only the required effect.

**1.1.8 — Selectively maximal action.** Keep the overall field/action at one extreme while locally protecting regions that need less action or locally adding substances that generate additional action only where needed.

## 1.2 Destruction of harmful Su-Fields (5)

**1.2.1 — Introduce a foreign third substance.** If direct contact is unnecessary, interpose a cheap/free `S3` to eliminate the harmful interaction while retaining the useful function.

**1.2.2 — Derive the third substance from existing substances.** When foreign additives are forbidden, create `S3` by modifying `S1`, `S2`, or the environment; a void, bubble, foam, coating, or phase-modified region may serve this role.

**1.2.3 — Add a sacrificial substance.** Introduce a substance that preferentially absorbs or receives the harmful field/effect.

**1.2.4 — Add a counteracting field.** Preserve required contact/useful action while a second field cancels, redirects, or converts the harmful action.

**1.2.5 — Switch off magnetic coupling.** Use a physical effect that removes ferromagnetic behavior, such as demagnetization or crossing the Curie temperature, when the harmful Su-Field is magnetic.

# Class 2 — Development of Su-Fields (23)

## 2.1 Transition to more sophisticated Su-Fields (2)

**2.1.1 — Transition to a chain Su-Field.** Turn one substance into a separately controlled Su-Field, creating a sequence of interacting Su-Fields.

**2.1.2 — Transition to a double Su-Field.** If replacing existing elements is unacceptable, add a second field to increase efficiency or controllability.

## 2.2 Intensification without adding new substances/fields (6)

**2.2.1 — Transition to a more controllable field.** Replace a poorly controlled field with one that permits finer engineering control.

**2.2.2 — Segment the substance.** Increase fragmentation/dispersion of `S1` or `S2` to gain area, responsiveness, or controllability.

**2.2.3 — Transition to capillary-porous structure.** Evolve solid structure through cavities/perforation toward designed capillary/porous systems, enabling new field and fluid interactions.

**2.2.4 — Dynamize the Su-Field.** Move from rigid/static structure or constant field toward flexible, adjustable, pulsed, or rapidly changing configurations.

**2.2.5 — Structure the field.** Replace a homogeneous field with a spatially and/or temporally structured field tailored to the required effect.

**2.2.6 — Structure the substance.** Replace a homogeneous substance with a spatially or temporally structured/graded substance.

## 2.3 Intensification through rhythm coordination (3)

**2.3.1 — Coordinate field and substance rhythms.** Match the applied field frequency/timing to the relevant natural frequency or process rhythm of the substance.

**2.3.2 — Coordinate rhythms of multiple fields.** Synchronize or deliberately relate multiple fields in a complex Su-Field.

**2.3.3 — Coordinate previously incompatible activities.** Perform one activity during the pauses or low-demand intervals of another, filling idle time without direct interference.

## 2.4 Fe-Fields and related intensified field systems (12)

**2.4.1 — Transition to a Proto-Fe-Field.** Introduce a ferromagnetic substance as a whole plus a magnetic field.

**2.4.2 — Transition to a Fe-Field.** Replace/add ferro-particles and use magnetic/electromagnetic fields; increase controllability by increasing particle and carrier-medium segmentation.

**2.4.3 — Use ferromagnetic fluids.** Use colloidal ferro-particle suspensions as highly controllable working substances.

**2.4.4 — Use capillary-porous structures in Fe-Fields.** Combine magnetic control with capillary/porous localization and transport.

**2.4.5 — Transition to a complex Fe-Field.** When replacing a principal substance is unacceptable, introduce ferromagnetic additives internally or externally.

**2.4.6 — Build the Fe-Field through the external environment.** Put the controllable magnetic/electrorheological material in the environment/supersystem instead of the protected system element.

**2.4.7 — Use physical effects to control the Fe-Field.** Exploit appropriate physical phenomena to change magnetic/field-responsive behavior.

**2.4.8 — Dynamize the Fe-Field.** Make the magnetic field or field-responsive structure flexible, variable, pulsed, adaptive, or reconfigurable.

**2.4.9 — Structure the Fe-Field.** Replace homogeneous magnetic interaction with spatially/temporally structured fields and/or substances.

**2.4.10 — Coordinate Fe-Field rhythms.** Match timing/frequency among magnetic field, particles, carrier, and system dynamics.

**2.4.11 — Transition to an E-Field.** When magnetic materials are unsuitable, use interacting direct/induced electrical currents and electromagnetic action; evolve the E-Field along analogous complexification/dynamization paths.

**2.4.12 — Use electrorheological liquids.** Where ferrofluids are unsuitable, use electrically active particulate suspensions whose rheology is controlled by electric fields.

# Class 3 — Transition to supersystem and microlevel (6)

## 3.1 Transition to bi- and poly-systems (5)

**3.1.1 — Transition to a bi-/poly-system.** Integrate the system with one or more systems to obtain a new quality.

**3.1.2 — Develop links within the bi-/poly-system.** Evolve links from absent/weak through rigid, flexible, and field-mediated forms as appropriate.

**3.1.3 — Increase differences among system elements.** Evolve from identical elements toward shifted characteristics, diverse elements, and complementary/opposite combinations.

**3.1.4 — Trim the bi-/poly-system.** Remove or redistribute components/functions after integration; a mature poly-system may collapse back toward a multifunctional monosystem.

**3.1.5 — Give system and parts opposite properties.** Distribute property `C` at one system level and `anti-C` at another to resolve a contradiction hierarchically.

## 3.2 Transition to microlevel (1)

**3.2.1 — Transition to microlevel.** Replace a macro-level system/part with particles, material structure, or lower-level substance capable of performing the function through interaction with a field.

# Class 4 — Standard solutions for measuring and detecting (17)

## 4.1 Bypasses (3)

**4.1.1 — Eliminate the need to measure/detect.** Change the operating principle so the required outcome no longer depends on directly knowing the quantity.

**4.1.2 — Measure a copy/image.** Replace direct work on the inaccessible object with measurement of a scaled image, model, signal, or surrogate.

**4.1.3 — Convert to successive-change detection.** Replace difficult absolute measurement with detection of changes, transitions, thresholds, or successive differences.

## 4.2 Create a measurement Su-Field (4)

**4.2.1 — Create a simple/double measurement Su-Field.** Introduce missing sensing substance/field so the output is an observable field.

**4.2.2 — Transition to a complex measurement Su-Field.** Introduce an easily detected additive/marker internally or externally.

**4.2.3 — Build the measurement Su-Field in the external environment.** Put the marker/additive in the environment when the system itself cannot be modified.

**4.2.4 — Generate the marker from environmental resources.** If additives cannot be introduced even into the environment, create detectable substances/effects by decomposing or changing the state of existing resources.

## 4.3 Intensify measurement Su-Fields (3)

**4.3.1 — Use scientific/physical effects.** Improve detection by selecting a phenomenon that converts the hidden quantity into an observable signal.

**4.3.2 — Use resonance of the controlled object.** Infer state/property from changes in resonant response when direct access is difficult.

**4.3.3 — Use resonance of an integrated external object.** Couple an auxiliary object to the system and infer system condition from the auxiliary object's natural-frequency changes.

## 4.4 Transition to measurement Fe-Fields (5)

**4.4.1 — Transition to measurement Proto-Fe-Fields.** Introduce a magnetic substance plus magnetic field to improve detectability.

**4.4.2 — Transition to a measurement Fe-Field.** Use ferromagnetic particles/additives and measure the associated magnetic response.

**4.4.3 — Transition to a complex measurement Fe-Field.** When replacing system substances is impossible, add ferromagnetic markers/additives to create an internal/external complex measuring Fe-Field.

**4.4.4 — Build the measuring Fe-Field in the external environment.** Put ferromagnetic particles/markers outside the protected system and sense their response to system state.

**4.4.5 — Use physical effects.** Increase measurement sensitivity through magnetic/scientific effects such as Curie-point, Barkhausen, magnetoelastic, or other validated phenomena.

## 4.5 Direction of measurement-system development (2)

**4.5.1 — Transition to measurement bi-/poly-systems.** Combine multiple sensing systems/modalities to improve reliability, range, or information content.

**4.5.2 — Transition to measuring derivatives.** Evolve from measuring the function itself to first/second derivatives or other more informative transformations when they reveal state more effectively.

# Class 5 — Standards on application of SISs (17)

## 5.1 Specifics of introducing substances (4)

**5.1.1 — Bypass when introducing a substance is restricted.** Use one or more sub-techniques: void instead of substance; field instead of substance; external instead of internal additive; tiny dose of highly active additive; local concentration of an ordinary additive; temporary additive; additive in a copy/model; additive released from a compound; generate the additive from the environment/object by decomposition or state change.

**5.1.2 — Split the product into interacting parts.** When the tool/additive cannot be changed or added, make parts of the product interact so the product itself supplies the required action.

**5.1.3 — Use the introduced substance only temporarily.** After performing its function, make it disappear, transform, or become indistinguishable from existing substance/environment.

**5.1.4 — Use inflatable structures or foam.** When a large amount of added substance is forbidden, use void-rich structures to provide bulk/function with little material.

## 5.2 Introducing fields (3)

**5.2.1 — Use existing internal fields first.** Exploit fields already carried/generated by system substances.

**5.2.2 — Use environmental fields.** Use gravity, ambient thermal/electrical/magnetic/mechanical fields, pressure, flow, radiation, etc., when appropriate.

**5.2.3 — Generate fields from existing substances.** Use system/environment substances as sources of the required field rather than adding a dedicated field generator.

## 5.3 Using phase transitions (5)

**5.3.1 — Change phase state of an existing substance.** Obtain new behavior without adding another substance.

**5.3.2 — Use a substance capable of two phase states.** Let operating conditions switch the working material between required states.

**5.3.3 — Use phenomena accompanying phase change.** Exploit latent heat, volume, solubility, conductivity, magnetism, optical change, etc.

**5.3.4 — Transition from single-phase to two-phase state.** Obtain dual/complementary properties by maintaining two phases simultaneously.

**5.3.5 — Use interactions between phases.** Exploit physical/chemical interaction at phase boundaries to increase system efficiency.

## 5.4 Specifics of using scientific effects (2)

**5.4.1 — Use reversible physical transformations.** If the object must alternate between states, use intrinsic reversible transformations (phase change, ionization/recombination, association/dissociation, etc.) rather than external replacement.

**5.4.2 — Strengthen the output field near a critical state.** Store energy or place a converter near a threshold so a weak input acts as a trigger for a stronger output.

## 5.5 Experimental standards (3)

**5.5.1 — Obtain particles by decomposition.** Generate required lower-structural-level particles by destroying a higher-level substance when direct generation is impractical.

**5.5.2 — Obtain particles by combination.** Generate required higher-level particles by combining lower-level particles when direct acquisition is impractical.

**5.5.3 — Use the nearest structural level.** For decomposition, destroy the nearest higher whole/excess level; for combination, complete the nearest lower incomplete level to minimize transformation cost.
