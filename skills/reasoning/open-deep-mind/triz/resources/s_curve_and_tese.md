## S-curve analysis

Treat each important MPV separately. A mature system on one MPV may be immature on another.

For MPV `m`:

```math
m = f(t)
```

Assess:

- absolute performance;
- first and second rate-of-improvement signals where data permit;
- effort/cost per marginal improvement;
- physical or architectural limits;
- number/severity of unresolved contradictions;
- market/production maturity where relevant;
- emergence of alternatives with a different principle of operation.

Classify provisionally:

```text
Stage 1 — emergence/prototype
Transition — scale-up/market-entry challenge
Stage 2 — rapid growth
Stage 3 — maturity / diminishing returns / limits
Stage 4 — decline, niche persistence, supersystem absorption, or replacement
```

Do not force a smooth sigmoid fit when the data do not support it.

## TESE sweep

After stage analysis, systematically inspect:

- increasing value;
- transition to supersystem;
- increasing degree of trimming;
- increasing completeness of components;
- decreasing human involvement where appropriate;
- flow enhancement;
- increasing coordination;
- increasing controllability;
- increasing dynamization;
- uneven development of components.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Prefer transitions supported by multiple independent signals rather than one trend name.

## New S-curve test

A candidate new principle of operation deserves a separate S-curve hypothesis when it:

- changes the dominant physical/technical mechanism;
- relaxes a mature system's binding limit;
- creates a new MPV frontier;
- requires different key resources or system architecture.

Compare the old and new curves under common functional and economic boundaries before declaring replacement.
