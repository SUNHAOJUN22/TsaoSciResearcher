# First Philosophy Engine / 第一哲学思考

## Purpose

First Philosophy does not begin by asking, “How do we solve the problem?” It asks:

> **What must be clarified, accepted, or justified before this can count as a coherent problem and before any solution can count as warranted?**

“First” means **foundational priority within an inquiry**, not chronological priority and not automatic metaphysical certainty.

This engine qualifies foundations across philosophy, science, engineering, strategy, policy, creative work, and personal decisions.

---

## 1. What the engine examines

A foundation is not only a “fact.” A serious inquiry may depend on eight dimensions:

\[
\mathcal F_{\Phi}
=
\{\mathcal S,\mathcal O,\mathcal K,\mathcal L,
\mathcal C,\mathcal B,\mathcal V,\mathcal P\}
\]

| Symbol | Lens | Core question |
|---|---|---|
| \(\mathcal S\) | Semantics / 语义 | What exactly do the key words mean? |
| \(\mathcal O\) | Ontology / 本体论 | What entities, processes, relations, or absences are being treated as real? |
| \(\mathcal K\) | Epistemology / 认识论 | What is known, how, by whom, and with what warrant? |
| \(\mathcal L\) | Logic / 逻辑 | What follows from what, and are the categories consistent? |
| \(\mathcal C\) | Causality and explanation / 因果与解释 | What produces, enables, constitutes, or explains the phenomenon? |
| \(\mathcal B\) | Boundary, scale, time / 边界尺度时间 | Where does the claim apply, and where does it stop? |
| \(\mathcal V\) | Axiology and ethics / 价值与伦理 | What matters, to whom, and who bears benefits or harms? |
| \(\mathcal P\) | Praxis / 实践 | What action is justified under the remaining uncertainty? |

A complete foundation need not resolve every philosophical dispute. It must make the active commitments explicit enough for the next stage.

## 2. The Φ8 protocol

### Φ0 — Suspend the inherited frame

Temporarily bracket, rather than erase:

- habitual terminology;
- default metrics;
- organizational authority;
- industry convention;
- the first proposed solution;
- the assumption that the stated problem is the real problem.

Ask:

- Who framed the question?
- What is excluded by this wording?
- What would the situation look like under a rival frame?
- Is this a problem of being, knowing, deciding, or acting?
- Does the question contain a false dichotomy?

**Output:** a neutral reformulation plus at least one rival formulation.

---

### Φ1 — Semantic audit

For every load-bearing term, create an entry:

| Term | Ordinary meaning | Technical meaning | Operational test | Exclusions | Ambiguity risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Use four definition types deliberately:

1. **Lexical:** how a community uses the term.
2. **Stipulative:** how this inquiry will use it.
3. **Operational:** how it will be observed or measured.
4. **Theoretical:** how it functions inside a model.

Tests:

- Can two participants agree verbally while using different meanings?
- Does the definition presuppose the conclusion?
- Is a continuous phenomenon forced into a false binary?
- Is a proxy being treated as the thing itself?
- Does the operational definition omit essential cases?

**Red blocker:** a central conclusion changes when a key term changes meaning.

---

### Φ2 — Ontology map

Classify what the inquiry assumes exists:

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Enduring object | person, component, molecule, institution |
| Process | learning, reaction, degradation, negotiation |
| Event | failure, purchase, phase transition |
| Property | mass, trust, intelligence, risk |
| Relation | ownership, correlation, dependence, responsibility |
| Structure | network, hierarchy, topology, grammar |
| Disposition | fragility, solubility, capability |
| Absence / counterfactual | missing safeguard, avoided harm, unrealized option |

Then ask:

- Is the target an object, process, relation, property, or construction?
- Are emergent entities reducible for this purpose, or only dependent?
- Is the same entity preserved across time and change?
- Are social categories treated as natural kinds without justification?
- Are model variables being reified as independent things?

**Output:** an ontology map and any contested entities.

---

### Φ3 — Epistemic audit

Classify every important claim by source and status.

| Claim | Access mode | Evidence | Status | Dependence | Error mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Direct observation | measurement, record, experience | data/source | verified/supported | instrument/observer | noise, bias |
| Inference | deduction, induction, abduction | premises | plausible/contested | model | underdetermination |
| Testimony | expert, document, institution | authority | supported/contested | credibility | outdated/error |
| Model output | simulation, forecast, fit | model + data | conditional | assumptions | model discrepancy |
| Value judgment | duty, preference, objective | reasons/stakeholders | explicit | normative frame | hidden trade-off |

Ask:

- What could the observer or instrument not detect?
- Is evidence independent, or repeated from one source?
- Does the claim exceed the measurement conditions?
- What is known only through a model?
- Which unknowns are decision-critical?
- What evidence would discriminate rival explanations?

**Output:** an epistemic status table and uncertainty frontier.

---

### Φ4 — Logical audit

Check:

- identity and non-contradiction under the same conditions;
- necessary versus sufficient conditions;
- universal versus existential claims;
- possibility versus actuality;
- correlation versus implication;
- descriptive “is” versus normative “ought”;
- part–whole and level-of-analysis errors;
- quantifier and scope shifts;
- circular definitions;
- self-defeating statements.

Use a minimal argument map:

```text
P1 [type/status]
P2 [type/status]
Rule [deduction / induction / abduction / analogy]
C  [scope and confidence]
```

For deduction, validity does not establish the truth of premises. For induction and abduction, state rival explanations and confidence.

**Red blocker:** the conclusion does not follow even if all premises are granted.

---

### Φ5 — Causality and explanation audit

Distinguish explanatory roles:

| Role | Question |
|---|---|
| Constitutive | What makes the phenomenon the kind of thing it is? |
| Material | What is it made from or instantiated in? |
| Structural / formal | What organization gives it its behavior? |
| Efficient / generative | What process produces the change? |
| Functional / purposive | What role or goal explains its persistence or design? |
| Enabling | What background condition makes it possible? |
| Triggering | What event initiates it? |
| Maintaining | What feedback keeps it going? |
| Preventive | What would block it? |

Do not collapse these into one generic “cause.”

For intervention questions, represent:

\[
X_i := f_i(\operatorname{Pa}_i,U_i)
\]

and ask whether the available data identify the effect of \(do(X=x)\), not merely \(P(Y\mid X=x)\).

For mechanism explanations, require:

\[
\text{entities} + \text{activities} + \text{organization}
\rightarrow \text{phenomenon}
\]

**Output:** causal/explanatory map, identification assumptions, and rival mechanism.

---

### Φ6 — Boundary, scale, and time audit

State explicitly:

- system boundary;
- environment;
- spatial scale;
- temporal scale and horizon;
- population or jurisdiction;
- initial state;
- path dependence;
- equilibrium or non-equilibrium status;
- open or closed system;
- reversible or irreversible decision.

Use a scale bridge table:

| From scale | To scale | Bridge variable/model | Information lost | Validation |
|---|---|---|---|---|

**Red blocker:** a conclusion crosses scales with no bridge.

---

### Φ7 — Value, ethics, and praxis audit

Separate:

\[
\text{descriptive model}
\quad\text{from}\quad
\text{normative objective}
\]

Create a value ledger:

| Stakeholder | Benefit | Harm | Right/duty | Voice/power | Distribution | Reversibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Ask:

- Who selected the objective function?
- Which values are treated as constraints rather than tradeable utilities?
- Who benefits, who pays, and who decides?
- Are present gains purchased with future or externalized harm?
- Is consent meaningful?
- Are vulnerable parties represented?
- Would the rule be acceptable if roles were reversed?
- What precaution is justified by uncertainty and irreversibility?

Then choose a praxis stance:

- act;
- run a discriminating test;
- delay;
- preserve options;
- consult affected parties;
- prohibit;
- monitor and revise.

**Output:** explicit value commitments and an action justified under uncertainty.

## 4. Philosophical method cards

### Aristotelian foundation analysis

Use when the inquiry asks what something is and why it behaves as it does.

1. classify the kind of being;
2. separate substance/object, property, process, and relation;
3. analyze material, structural, generative, and functional explanations;
4. identify potentiality versus realized state;
5. specify conditions of identity through change.

Do not treat the four causes as equivalent to a modern interventionist causal graph.

### Cartesian methodic doubt

Use when inherited beliefs or authority dominate.

1. list beliefs;
2. identify what could be mistaken;
3. retain only what survives the required doubt standard;
4. rebuild in small explicit steps.

Do not demand absolute certainty for ordinary empirical decisions.

### Kantian conditions-of-possibility analysis

Use when asking what must be true for an experience, measurement, institution, or capability to be possible.

1. establish the target phenomenon;
2. propose necessary conditions;
3. test whether alternatives could also enable it;
4. distinguish constitutive conditions from contingent implementation.

Avoid treating one sufficient condition as uniquely necessary.

### Phenomenological reduction

Use when lived experience, interface, observation, or meaning is central.

1. bracket premature causal claims;
2. describe how the phenomenon appears;
3. separate first-person structure from third-person explanation;
4. analyze attention, temporality, embodiment, and context;
5. reconnect description to empirical models without erasing either level.

### Hermeneutic iteration

Use for texts, institutions, historical systems, and ambiguous evidence.

1. interpret parts through the whole;
2. revise the whole through anomalous parts;
3. expose the interpreter’s prior horizon;
4. compare rival coherent readings;
5. preserve context and historical distance.

### Ethical-first analysis

Use when responsibility, rights, harm, or asymmetrical power is foundational.

1. identify affected others;
2. map duties before utility maximization;
3. test consent, reversibility, and distribution;
4. preserve non-tradeable protections;
5. make residual moral disagreement explicit.

### Naturalized and pragmatic audit

Use when a supposedly absolute foundation can be investigated empirically.

1. ask how beliefs and categories are actually formed;
2. compare their predictive and practical consequences;
3. test reliability across environments;
4. revise the framework as part of science rather than outside it.

Do not reduce truth to convenience alone.

## 5. First Philosophy quality tests

A foundation passes provisionally only if:

- terms remain stable;
- the ontology fits the explanatory task;
- evidence access is explicit;
- inference form is valid or calibrated;
- causal roles are distinguished;
- boundaries and scale are stated;
- values are not disguised as facts;
- a rival frame has been considered;
- unresolved commitments are visible;
- the foundation can be revised without hiding the change.

## 6. Common failure modes

| Failure | Symptom | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| Infinite regress | every reason demands another with no stopping rule | state a task-relative stopping criterion |
| Dogmatic foundation | “obvious” premise with no audit | classify and justify the premise |
| Category mistake | treating a process as an object or a norm as a fact | rebuild ontology and semantics |
| Reification | treating a metric/model variable as the real phenomenon | restore operational and theoretical distinction |
| Reductionism | lower-level account declared sufficient for every purpose | test explanatory adequacy at each level |
| Relativism by exhaustion | disagreement taken to mean no claim can be better supported | compare evidence, coherence, and consequences |
| Hidden normativity | optimization target presented as natural law | create value ledger |
| Frame capture | stakeholder wording determines all conclusions | add rival frame and neutral reformulation |

For additional diagnostics, load [`../references/failure-modes.md`](../references/failure-modes.md).

## 7. Handoff to First Principles

Transfer only foundations labeled:

- **accepted**;
- **accepted conditionally**;
- **unresolved but non-blocking**.

Do not transfer a disputed claim without its status.

The handoff package is:

\[
\mathcal H_{\Phi\rightarrow P}
=
\{\text{definitions},\text{ontology},\text{evidence status},
\text{logic},\text{causal commitments},\text{boundary},
\text{values},\text{unknowns}\}
\]
